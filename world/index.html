Sprawl · world atlas

Every biome, and how they meet

The world of Sprawl is forged from a seed. Climate chooses the land, height stacks it into ladders, and a table of neighbours decides who may border whom. Here is all of it, measured over real generated worlds.

Where each biome lives

Each biome sits at the median temperature and height of every cell it holds across the surveyed worlds. The bubble's size is its share of the land. Climb the chart and the country changes: woods give way to pine, then to the last stunted trees, then to bare rock. Tap a bubble to open its field card.

Height is the forge's land height (0 at the shore, 1 at the highest peak), on a square-root scale so the crowded lowlands get room. The air cools 22 °C from shore to summit, so the same biome sits lower in the cold north than in the warm south.

Who meets whom

Every land biome against every other. A filled square means they can border each other, and the brighter it is, the wider the band where one blends into the other. A red mark means they must never touch: the adjacency rules convert one side into a buffer first. Hover or tap a square to read the rule.

The families

rendered in the Biome Lab · placeholder colours

Biomes belong to families that share a look, and a family sets which neighbours a biome may have. The pictures are the Biome Lab's placeholder renders: the painted art sets come later, in the game's dark painterly style.

When two can't touch

Some pairs would look wrong side by side, such as a glacier against grassland or jungle against taiga. After the climate is laid down, the forge finds every such pair within two cells. The higher-ranked side then gives way to a buffer biome that can touch both, and it repeats until none are left, usually within two to five rounds. That is how a world grows ecotones instead of seams.
