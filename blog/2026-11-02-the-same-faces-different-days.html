The same faces, different days

Oakwell should be somewhere you can recognise. A different adventure should not require Ysolde to become a different person, or a familiar road to lead to a town that has moved overnight.

The next implementation for review begins to separate the setting from the history that happens there. The county definition keeps its twelve settlements, their streets, rivers and main road connections. Highbury keeps its place too, even though its layout began as a generator experiment. Countryside details can still vary around those anchors.

The same distinction reaches Oakwell's three established residents. New histories start with the same Ysolde, Hamon and Odette, including their starting natures. Their choices can still respond to different circumstances. Familiarity gives those differences someone to happen to.

An existing history deserves the same care. Older saves keep the people they started with, including their original personalities. Remembered help, changed households, losses and belongings must survive Continue. The starting definition is not permission to wind a person's life back to the beginning.

There is still a seam to finish. Oakwell's compound inn and the separate town launches have older layout and save rules. Those need a careful transition before the entire setting can be called permanent in ordinary play. The other towns also need real resident lives behind the figures already standing in them.

This is a foundation for a day in Oakwell, not that whole day delivered. The next proof is a connected journey: notice a need, prepare, leave town, return and see what changed. Then close the game, come back, and find that the day still mattered.
