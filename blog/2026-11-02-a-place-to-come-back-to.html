A place to come back to

2 November 2026

Oakwell will be there when you begin again. The question is what will happen to its people this time.

We have made a decision about the kind of world Sprawl is becoming. Oakwell and its neighbouring towns will be recurring places in the game, with recognisable streets, working buildings and a core cast of familiar people. Marlbury belongs to that setting too.

Until now, the plan treated the first county as the place to prove a method for generating entirely different towns. Those tools are still useful. But the county itself is becoming somewhere worth keeping.

Familiar faces, different circumstances

Knowing a place gives its changes weight. You can learn where the mill stands, who works there and which road brings grain into town. When something goes wrong, you have a reason to notice.

The aim is for different stories to grow from the same people and consistent rules. A shortage, an unsafe road or your own decision to help can change what someone does next. Their response should follow what they need, what they know and what has actually happened.

These are possibilities we need to prove through play. They are not promises that every one of those situations is already working.

Keeping the cast does not mean freezing their lives. Supported changes during a game should remain changed: a move, a lost friendship, a closed workplace or a remembered kindness. Starting again begins a new history. Continuing a save keeps the history you made.

The land between the towns

There is still room for countryside to vary between new games. Woodland detail, minor trails, resources and suitable rural buildings can come from a saved seed. Once you find somewhere, it must remain there when you return.

The geography has responsibilities. The mill needs its river. A fishing village needs its shore. Roads must still reach their bridges and town entrances. Variation has to respect those connections and blend naturally into the surrounding land.

We also want each settlement to earn its own appearance. Climate, local materials, trade and terrain should shape its buildings and streets. Making Oakwell convincing does not finish the work of making a different architectural family convincing.

First, a day in Oakwell

The next useful milestone is a connected session of roughly half an hour to three quarters of an hour. You notice a real need, prepare for an outing, leave town, face or avoid danger, and return to see what your choice changed.

There must be a way back from a setback, and saving and continuing must preserve the result. The people around you need readable responses to what happens, including a sensible reaction when danger interrupts their day.

We will begin with Oakwell and its nearby countryside. That is enough to test whether the journey holds together. A neighbouring town comes after that; finishing every settlement and biome is not a condition for learning from the first session.

Before inviting trusted testers, the same journey also needs a dependable desktop package, clear controls, tested recovery and measured performance on the intended hardware. A successful automated check alone cannot tell us whether someone knows what to do or cares about the outcome.

This update sets the direction and the work ahead. It does not announce a finished playtest build. The goal is a place you can learn, people you can remember, and consequences that give you a reason to come back.
