One vocation, many skills

A traveller can learn to gather useful plants without deciding that gathering is their life's work. A warrior can prepare a field remedy. An apothecary can still become a careful picker. The profession work being recovered for review starts from that distinction: ordinary practice stays open, while a deliberately chosen vocation gives a character a deeper direction.

A choice that keeps its meaning

Letting a character collect every profession makes experimentation easy, but eventually gives every seasoned traveller the same collection of specialist techniques. Locking ordinary activities behind one profession creates a different problem: a person who chose another craft cannot do a simple useful task even after practising it.

The proposed middle ground is one major profession, with ordinary skills that keep growing through use. Trying an activity comes before committing. The character's combat abilities remain a separate choice. A profession is a direction to develop, rather than a replacement for the rest of the character.

That leaves room for trade to matter. Two capable travellers can share ordinary knowledge yet value each other's specialised products. Buying a carefully made remedy does not mean learning the method that produced it.

Start with the rules of a useful craft

The recovered foundation concentrates on the journey from plant to field remedy. Its rules model Herbalist and Apothecary as alternative major professions within that connected activity. Gathering and preparation can advance ordinary practice before choosing either one. This foundation is not yet connected to the current town's player controls.

The specialist choices aim to change actual production. A careful harvesting method can recover more usable material; a preservation method can help prepare still-viable aged ingredients. An apothecary can favour stronger remedies or more output from a preparation. A choice should describe its effect before it becomes permanent.

Practice needs to come from successful work. Reading a menu, reopening an offer, cancelling an attempt or loading a save must not advance a profession. Products also need to keep their actual properties when traded or saved, rather than changing because their maker later learned something new.

Recovering the unfinished work

This is work in review, not a released feature. The earlier branch was left without a pull request while the town and its character systems continued to change. Its old controls and save integration cannot simply be brought back alongside the newer systems. The first recovery increment isolates the profession and crafting rules so they can be checked without replacing today's character ownership.

The playable integration remains ahead. It must use the town's current inventory, money and character save transaction, work alongside the herbalist service already there, and give the profession menu a suitable control. A separate copy of a traveller's goods or health would make the craft unreliable, especially after returning to a saved game.

The design also has clear limits. This first craft does not make smithing, cooking or every other profession playable. It has authored specialist choices rather than an unlimited generator of new powers. Retraining is not part of the initial proposal, so the commitment needs a clear explanation before it is accepted.

The next review needs to show the full sequence in the town: gather, practise, choose, prepare, use and trade, then save and return. Those checks matter as much as the profession menu. The aim is a choice that makes a character distinctive throughout play, with ordinary competence still earned by doing the work.
